# Two-agent workflow — observed case study

**Observation date:** 12 November 2026. **Phase 1 governance amendments followed the recorded production events.** This public file contains aggregated figures only; no recipients, private tracking IDs, manuscript text, Gmail links or passwords.

The +5 cumulative gain came primarily from correcting interpretation of **existing approved document categories**, not discovering five new agents. A strong literary match can be held for an unavailable attachment or an unresolved historical agency restriction; the later Phase 1 design persists the two judgements separately.

The five submitted recipients and one remaining held recipient represent **5/6 (83.3%) research-prequalified handoff conversion** for this small pilot. This is not a controlled comparison with a single-agent alternative, not an agency acceptance rate and not a forecast.

## Operational recovery observations

- An initially incorrect two-attachment connector invocation prevented drafting. An unsent diagnostic with two verified document files established the correct interface; later actual sends were supported by provider confirmation.
- A form upload initially failed before submission and was documented as **proven not delivered**. A separately authorised fresh attempt succeeded after scoped owner permission.
- Advisory versus mandatory named-addressing was ambiguous with an immutable, owner-attested human-authored query. One case was submitted under explicit owner direction; another stayed held. Transmission evidence does not prove all agency preferences were met.
- Five **older** ambiguous sends remained protected from repeat delivery. They are not five new failures from the two-agent trial.

## Phase 1 governance improvement — retrospective, not new measured production

After the recorded submissions, the private handoff added persistent literary fit (AC) and preliminary readiness (AD) classifications. The 15 historical rows were classified conservatively from their existing evidence: **11 FIT-QUALIFIED; six historically research-ready**, with five already sent and one independently held. A **30-day** official-route freshness gate, clearer revision checking and website prompt-injection boundaries were also introduced.

These corrections were **not** a fourth send run or a new prospecting pilot. Backfilled labels are not proof of scoring consistency. The ten-case independent human calibration remains future work.

| New Phase 1 controls improved future throughput | **Subsequently observed in a separate supervised 13 November run; not a causal estimate** |
| Total owner time, production cost and model tokens | `UNAVAILABLE` |

**Engineering lesson:** The original bottlenecks were not simply prospect discovery. Separate fit quality from execution readiness, then validate tool capability before consuming external send attempts. Preserve controls against duplicate or uncertain delivery rather than optimising only for sends per hour.
